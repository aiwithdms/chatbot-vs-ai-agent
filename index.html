CHATBOT VS AI AGENT

From Answering Questions to Progressing Business Tasks

One fictional Indian e-commerce complaint. Three different systems. By the end you will see how a chatbot, rule-based automation and an AI agent differ in practice.

All companies, customers, orders and results in this experience are fictional and simulated. Nothing connects to a real system.

Scene 2 of 7 · Approach A

THE CHATBOT RESPONDS.

Simulated support chat

What the chatbot does

  • Understands the customer's question.
  • Provides relevant information.
  • Explains the available process.

What this example does not demonstrate

  • Retrieving order information.
  • Checking return eligibility.
  • Preparing a refund request.

Important: this is a deliberately simple chatbot. Modern chatbots can also connect to tools and perform actions. A conversational interface alone does not decide whether a system is an agent.

Scene 3 of 7 · Approach B

THE WORKFLOW FOLLOWS THE RULES.

Click any stage to see its purpose.
IF complaint category = “Damaged product”
THEN create a support ticket and send the return instructions.

Strengths

  • Predictable.
  • Consistent.
  • Useful for repetitive tasks.
  • Easy to audit when rules are properly maintained.

Limitations

  • Depends on configured rules.
  • May require additional rules for unexpected cases.
  • Does not automatically understand every new situation.

Rule-based automation is often the best choice for simple, predictable processes.

Scene 4 of 7 · Approach C

THE AI AGENT WORKS TOWARDS A GOAL.

    Sample agent response (simulated)

    “I'm sorry the product arrived damaged. I have reviewed the available order information in this demonstration. A photograph of the damaged jar is still required under the example return process. I recommend requesting the photograph before proceeding with the replacement or refund assessment.”
